NPCI — UPI CHARGEBACK, RE-PRESENTMENT AND ARBITRATION

Issuer: NPCI   

Note: synthetic summary prepared for the SENTINEL coursework corpus. It paraphrases public guidance and is not an official RBI or NPCI text.   

1. Chargeback
The remitter bank can raise a chargeback on behalf of its customer for reasons such as goods or services not delivered, a duplicate debit, a debit without a matching credit, or an amount different from what was agreed. A chargeback must be raised within the allowed window after the transaction date.

2.  Re-presentment
The beneficiary bank, usually on behalf of the merchant, can contest the chargeback by re-presenting the transaction with evidence such as delivery proof, a signed invoice or service logs.  The remitter bank then reviews the evidence.

3. Pre-arbitration and arbitration
If the parties still disagree, the case goes to pre-arbitration and then to arbitration by the NPCI committee, which decides based on the evidence submitted by both banks.   

4.  Good practice
Fraud teams should tag chargebacks that look like first-party misuse, such as a customer who disputes a delivery that was signed for.  Repeated chargebacks by the same customer across merchants should feed into risk scoring.
